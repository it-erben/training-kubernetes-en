k3s, RKE2, and Rancher Manager

The three often get lumped together. In fact, k3s and RKE2 are two Kubernetes distributions, and Rancher Manager sits above them as a control plane. Here's how they fit together:

Control plane
Rancher Manager
Not a distribution, but a management layer on top of the clusters. Rolls out k3s and RKE2 clusters, imports external k8s, and bundles access, permissions, monitoring, and GitOps (Fleet). Runs on a cluster itself.
Distribution

k3s

  • A ~70 MB binary, runs as one systemd service
  • State in SQLite by default, etcd works too
  • Traefik, ServiceLB, and local-path are already included
  • Made for edge, IoT, CI, and laptops, ARM included
Distribution

RKE2

  • Ships CIS-hardened and FIPS-compliant, no rework needed
  • Keeps its state in etcd
  • Uses containerd and SELinux instead of Docker
  • For production, government, and air-gapped setups. Rancher's default
Imported

External clusters

  • EKS, AKS, GKE, and any other CNCF k8s
  • Imported via an agent, not built by Rancher
  • They show up in the same dashboard as k3s and RKE2
  • One place for all clusters, wherever they come from
